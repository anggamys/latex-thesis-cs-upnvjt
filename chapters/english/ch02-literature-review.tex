\subsection{Sentiment Analysis}

Sentiment analysis is a field of study that analyzes opinions, sentiments, evaluations, attitudes, and emotions toward entities such as products, services, organizations, or topics \cite{feldman2013}. It falls under Natural Language Processing (NLP) and text mining. The main objective is to determine the polarity of the text: positive, negative, or neutral.

\subsection{Text Preprocessing}

Raw review text contains noise that can degrade classification performance. Common preprocessing stages include:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Case Folding}: Converting all letters to lowercase.
  \item \textbf{Tokenization}: Splitting sentences into word tokens.
  \item \textbf{Filtering}: Removing meaningless words (stopwords) and punctuation.
  \item \textbf{Stemming}: Converting affixed words to their base form.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{TF-IDF}

TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency) is a feature weighting method that assigns a value to each word based on how often it appears in a document and how rarely it appears across all documents \cite{manning2008}. The TF-IDF weight is calculated using the equation:

\begin{equation}
\text{TF-IDF}(t,d) = \text{TF}(t,d) \times \text{IDF}(t)
\end{equation}

where $\text{TF}(t,d)$ is the frequency of word $t$ in document $d$, and $\text{IDF}(t)$ is calculated as:

\begin{equation}
\text{IDF}(t) = \log\left(\frac{N}{\text{DF}(t)}\right)
\end{equation}

where $N$ is the total number of documents and $\text{DF}(t)$ is the number of documents containing word $t$.

\subsection{Na\"ive Bayes Classifier}

The Na\"ive Bayes Classifier is a probabilistic classification method based on Bayes' Theorem with the assumption of feature independence \cite{han2012}. Although the independence assumption is rarely satisfied in the real world, NBC has proven effective in text classification. Bayes' Theorem is expressed as:

\begin{equation}
P(C \mid X) = \frac{P(X \mid C) \cdot P(C)}{P(X)}
\end{equation}

where $P(C \mid X)$ is the probability of document $X$ belonging to class $C$, $P(X \mid C)$ is the probability of document $X$ occurring in class $C$, $P(C)$ is the prior probability of class $C$, and $P(X)$ is the probability of document $X$ occurring.
